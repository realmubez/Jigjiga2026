# **Muqmad: The Legendary Traveler’s Food**
### **The Original Nomadic Innovation**
Before modern refrigeration and canned goods, the Somali nomads needed a way to carry protein across vast, hot deserts without it spoiling. The solution was **Muqmad**. By dehydrating high-quality meat and sealing it in pure clarified butter, they created a food that remains fresh for months, even in the scorching heat of the Jigjiga plains.
### **The Craft: How it is Made**
The preparation of Muqmad is a labor of love that takes days. It is a traditional skill passed down from mothers to daughters.
 1. **Selection:** Lean cuts of beef or camel meat are sliced into thin strips.
 2. **Drying:** The strips are sun-dried or air-dried until they become jerky.
 3. **Dicing:** The dried meat is cut into tiny, bite-sized cubes.
 4. **Deep Frying:** The cubes are fried in **Subag** (clarified butter) with a blend of spices like cardamom and cloves until they are crispy.
 5. **Preservation:** The meat and the butter are poured into a **Haandey** (a specially carved wooden container). Once the butter cools and solidifies, it creates an airtight seal that prevents spoilage.
### **The Flavor Profile**
Muqmad has a unique taste that is both savory and rich. Because it is cooked in clarified butter, it has a deep, nutty aroma. The texture is a delightful contrast—crunchy on the outside but tender on the inside. When you take a bite, the spices release a warm, earthy flavor that defines the Somali breakfast experience.
### **How to Eat Muqmad Like a Local**
While it was designed for survival, today it is a luxury treat enjoyed in the best homes in Jigjiga.
 * **The Breakfast Duo:** Muqmad is almost always served with **Anjero**. You take a piece of Anjero, scoop up a few cubes of meat along with some of the solidified butter, and enjoy.
 * **The Sweet & Salty Mix:** Some people like to sprinkle a little sugar over the Muqmad and Anjero for a unique flavor combination.
 * **The Gift:** Because it lasts so long, Muqmad is the most common gift sent to Somali students or family members living abroad. It is literally "home in a jar."
### **A Symbol of High Status**
In Jigjiga, serving Muqmad to a guest is a sign of high respect. Because it requires a large amount of meat and expensive clarified butter to produce a small batch, it is considered a premium food. If you are invited to a home and served Muqmad, you know you are a highly valued guest.
### **Quick Facts Sidebar:**
 * **Other Name:** Often called *Odkac* in certain regions.
 * **Shelf Life:** Up to a year if kept in a traditional airtight container.
 * **Primary Ingredients:** Beef or Camel meat, Subag (Clarified Butter), and Xawaash (Spices).
 * **The Tradition:** Traditionally prepared for weddings to ensure the new couple has high-energy food for their first weeks together.
