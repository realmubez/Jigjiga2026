# **Dhaanto: The Heartbeat of the Somali Region**
### **The Soul of Somali Folklore**
Dhaanto is more than just a dance; it is the ultimate expression of Somali identity, joy, and storytelling. Originating in the Somali Region of Ethiopia, it has evolved from a nomadic celebration into a world-famous art form. In the streets and squares of Jigjiga, the sound of the Dhaanto beat is the signal that a celebration has truly begun.
### **The Nomadic Roots**
Historically, Dhaanto was created by nomadic camel herders. After a long day of traveling or during the rainy season (*Gu*), nomads would gather around a fire to sing and dance. The rhythmic "thumping" of the feet and the synchronized clapping were designed to mirror the steady, powerful heartbeat of a camel walking through the desert. It was a way to stay connected, share news, and celebrate life in the wild.
### **The Art of the Move: Rhythm and Style**
What makes Dhaanto unique is its structure. Unlike many dances that require instruments, traditional Dhaanto relies entirely on the human body:
 * **The Clap:** A sharp, rhythmic clapping that sets the tempo.
 * **The Step:** A synchronized jumping and stomping movement that requires perfect timing among the group.
 * **The Chant:** A lead singer (usually a man or woman with a powerful voice) starts a verse, and the entire group responds in a melodic chorus.
   The movements are graceful yet energetic, often involving the tossing of traditional sticks (*Bakoorad*) or the waving of colorful shawls.
### **Dhaanto as a Political Tool**
During the era of **Sayid Mohamed Abdullah Hassan** and the Dervish movement, Dhaanto took on a new meaning. It was used to spread messages of resistance and unity. Poets would hide secret messages in their lyrics to communicate across the region. Even today, many Dhaanto songs are deeply patriotic, praising the beauty of the land and the bravery of the people.
### **The Modern Revival**
Today, Dhaanto has moved from the nomadic campfire to the global stage. Modern artists have added instruments like the guitar, keyboard, and drums, creating a "Pop-Dhaanto" style that is popular in clubs and weddings from Jigjiga to London and Minneapolis. However, in the rural areas surrounding Jigjiga, the traditional "voice and clap" version remains the most authentic and respected form of the dance.
### **Experience it in Jigjiga**
If you visit Jigjiga during a wedding, a public holiday, or a cultural festival, you will inevitably see a Dhaanto circle. Visitors are often encouraged to join in—as long as you can keep the beat! It is a powerful symbol of the hospitality and high-energy culture that defines our city.
### **Quick Facts Sidebar:**
 * **Origin:** Somali Region (Ogaden), 19th Century.
 * **Style:** Rhythmic stomping, clapping, and call-and-response singing.
 * **Instruments:** Traditionally none (vocals and hands only); modernly includes strings and percussion.
 * **Occasions:** Weddings, Eids, Independence days, and cultural festivals.
