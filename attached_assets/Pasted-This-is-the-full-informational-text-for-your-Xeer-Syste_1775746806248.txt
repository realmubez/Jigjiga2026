# **The Xeer System: The Unwritten Constitution**
### **The Foundation of Somali Justice**
The Xeer (pronounced *HAY-er*) is one of the oldest polycentric legal systems in the world. Long before modern courts and written constitutions, the Somali people developed this sophisticated system of customary law. It is not written in books; instead, it is passed down through generations in the minds of the elders. It is the "social contract" that keeps families, clans, and the entire city of Jigjiga in a state of peace and mutual respect.
### **The Power of the Great Tree (Geedka Shirka)**
The Xeer does not take place in a cold, stone building. Traditionally, justice happens under the shade of a large tree, known as the *Geedka Shirka*. Here, there are no professional lawyers; instead, the elders from different families sit together in a circle. In this space, every man is equal, and the goal is not just to "punish" but to reach a consensus that restores balance to the community.
### **Compensation Over Prison: The "Diya" Concept**
Unlike Western law, which often focuses on jail time, the Xeer focuses on **Restorative Justice**. One of its most famous pillars is *Diya* (blood money). If a crime is committed, the offender’s family or clan pays compensation to the victim's family. This prevents cycles of revenge and ensures that the victim is supported while the offender is held accountable by their own community.
### **The Protectors of the Law: The Odayaal (Elders)**
The judges of the Xeer are the *Odayaal*—respected elders chosen for their wisdom, knowledge of history, and fair-mindedness. They are the living libraries of the law. When a dispute arises over land, water, or business in Jigjiga, the Odayaal use their deep understanding of the Xeer to negotiate a solution that satisfies both sides, ensuring that no one leaves the "Tree" feeling cheated.
### **Xeer in the Modern World**
In a modern city like Jigjiga, you might think the Xeer is a thing of the past—but it is more alive than ever. Even today, the modern legal system often works alongside the Xeer. Major community issues are frequently settled by the "Guurti" (Council of Elders) because the people trust the wisdom of the Xeer to provide lasting peace that a modern courtroom sometimes cannot. It is the heartbeat of Somali stability.
### **Quick Facts Sidebar:**
 * **Type of Law:** Customary / Oral Tradition
 * **Primary Goal:** Peace, Consensus, and Compensation
 * **Key Venue:** Under the Shade of the Acacia Tree
 * **The Motto:** *Soomaali waxay ku maahmaahdaa: "Xeer waa la xidhaa, xariggana waa la furayaa"* (Somali Proverb: "Law is tied, and the rope is untied" — meaning law provides the structure for freedom).
