The **Jigjiga Camel Market** (Suuqa Geela) is perhaps the most authentic and high-energy destination in the city. It is not just a place for buying and selling; it is the beating heart of the Somali Region’s economy and a window into the ancient nomadic way of life that continues to thrive in the 21st century.
# **Jigjiga Camel Market: The Pulse of the Desert**
### **The Largest Livestock Hub in the Horn**
Jigjiga’s camel market is legendary. As one of the largest livestock trading centers in East Africa, it serves as a massive crossroads for traders coming from across the Somali Region, Somaliland, and deeper into the Ethiopian highlands. On any given day, thousands of camels are brought here, creating a sea of humps and a symphony of sounds that you won't find anywhere else.
### **The Art of the Trade**
Watching a deal happen at the market is like watching a masterclass in negotiation.
 * **The "Handshake" Deal:** Traders often negotiate through coded hand signals under a cloth to keep the price secret from competitors.
 * **The Judgment:** Buyers look at the size of the hump (which stores fat and indicates health), the strength of the legs, and the texture of the coat.
 * **The Branding:** Each camel is marked with a unique tribal or family brand (*Sumad*) burned into its skin, which tells the story of its origin and lineage.
### **Why the Camel?**
To understand this landmark, you must understand the animal. To the Somali people, the camel is "White Gold."
 1. **Wealth:** A family’s status is often measured by the number of camels they own.
 2. **Survival:** In the semi-arid landscape surrounding Jigjiga, the camel is the only animal that can provide milk and transport through long droughts.
 3. **Culture:** The camel is the subject of the most famous Somali poems and songs. It is a symbol of nobility, patience, and endurance.
### **The Sensory Experience**
A visit to the market is a sensory overload in the best way possible:
 * **The Sight:** A horizon filled with hundreds of camels, dusty plains, and traders in colorful *Macawiis* (sarongs) and turbans.
 * **The Sound:** The deep grumbling of the camels mixed with the loud, rhythmic chanting of the traders as they call out prices.
 * **The Taste:** On the outskirts of the market, you can find small stalls selling the freshest **Caano Geel** (camel milk) served in traditional smoked wooden vessels.
### **The Market Beyond Camels**
While camels are the stars, the market also hosts thousands of goats, sheep, and cattle. Surrounding the livestock area is a secondary market where you can buy the tools of the nomadic trade:
 * **Traditional Gear:** Hand-carved wooden bells (*Koor*), intricate ropes, and woven camel saddles.
 * **Nomadic Fashion:** High-quality leather sandals and traditional sticks (*Bakoorad*) used by herders.
### **Quick Facts for Your Visitor:**
 * **Location:** On the outskirts of the city, easily accessible by a short bajaj (auto-rickshaw) ride.
 * **Best Time to Visit:** Early morning (between 7:00 AM and 10:00 AM) is when the trading is most intense and the temperature is cool.
 * **Photography:** Most traders are happy to have their camels photographed, but it is polite to ask for permission before taking close-up shots of the people.
 * **The "Milk Test":** If you’re brave, try the fresh camel milk sold nearby—it’s the true "energy drink" of Jigjiga.
