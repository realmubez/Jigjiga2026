# **Bariis Mindi: The King of Somali Cuisine**
### **More Than Just Rice**
Bariis Mindi is the ultimate symbol of hospitality and celebration in Jigjiga. While rice is a staple in many cultures, the Somali version is a culinary masterpiece defined by its aromatic scent, vibrant colors, and the "Mindi" (the method of serving it with meat). It is the dish served at every wedding, graduation, and high-level welcoming ceremony in the city.
### **The Secret is in the Spices (Xawaash)**
The soul of Bariis Mindi lies in the **Xawaash**—a hand-ground blend of spices that gives the rice its golden hue and incredible fragrance.
 * **The Mix:** Cumin, coriander, turmeric, cloves, cardamom, and cinnamon are toasted and ground together.
 * **The Scent:** When the rice is cooking, the smell fills the entire neighborhood. This is why Somalis say, *"Bariis bilaa xawaash ah waa sidii guri bilaa daaqad ah"* (Rice without spice is like a house without windows).
### **The Cooking Process: Slow and Fragrant**
The rice used is always high-quality, long-grain Basmati. It is first sautéed with onions, garlic, and the spice blend before being simmered in a rich meat broth. To add a touch of sweetness and texture, it is often topped with:
 * **Garnish:** Sautéed raisins, sliced peppers, and caramelized onions.
 * **Color:** Often, a touch of organic food coloring is used to create a beautiful "multi-color" effect of white, yellow, and orange grains.
### **The Star of the Show: The Meat (Hilib)**
A Bariis Mindi is never complete without the meat. In Jigjiga, you have two primary choices:
 1. **Hilib Ari (Goat Meat):** Tender chunks of goat, often slow-roasted until the meat falls off the bone.
 2. **Hilib Geel (Camel Meat):** The regional favorite. Camel meat is lean and has a deep, savory flavor that pairs perfectly with the spiced rice.
   The meat is usually placed right on top of the rice, allowing the juices to soak into the grains.
### **The Perfect Sides**
To eat Bariis Mindi like a true local in Jigjiga, you need the "The Trio":
 * **Moos (Banana):** It is a mandatory tradition to eat a banana with your rice. You slice it and mix it into the grains for a creamy, sweet contrast.
 * **Basbaas (Hot Sauce):** A fresh, spicy green sauce made from chilies, cilantro, and lime.
 * **Salad:** A simple mix of finely chopped tomatoes, onions, and lettuce to refresh the palate.
### **The Tradition of Sharing**
Bariis Mindi is traditionally served on a large communal platter. Families and friends sit together, often on the floor on a clean mat, and eat with their right hands. This method of eating symbolizes unity, equality, and the strength of the community. In Jigjiga, sharing a plate of Bariis is the fastest way to turn a stranger into a friend.
### **Quick Facts Sidebar:**
 * **Main Ingredients:** Basmati rice, Xawaash spices, raisins, onions.
 * **Traditional Pairing:** Banana and fresh lime juice.
 * **Best Time to Eat:** Friday lunch (the biggest meal of the week).
 * **Regional Variation:** Jigjiga is famous for using extra cardamom and cinnamon compared to coastal regions.
